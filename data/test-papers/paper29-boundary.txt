The Impact of Social Media Influencers on Teenage Purchasing Decisions
AP Research
April 2025
Word Count: 3,412
Introduction
Social media influencers have become one of the most powerful forces in modern marketing. Unlike traditional celebrities who promote products through formal advertising contracts, social media influencers build authentic-seeming relationships with their followers and promote products through what appears to be personal recommendation rather than paid advertising. Teenagers are particularly susceptible to influencer marketing because they spend more time on social media than any other age group, they are in a developmental stage characterized by heightened sensitivity to peer opinion and social comparison, and they have significant purchasing power both directly through their own spending and indirectly through their influence on household purchases. Research consistently shows that teenagers who follow social media influencers are more likely to purchase promoted products than those who do not follow influencers, but the psychological mechanisms driving this effect and the role of perceived authenticity specifically have not been fully examined. This paper investigates the following question: Do teenagers who perceive social media influencers as authentic make more influencer-driven purchases than teenagers who perceive influencers as inauthentic?
Literature Review
Social Media Influencers and Marketing
Social media influencer marketing has grown rapidly over the past decade. Freberg et al. (2011) conducted one of the first studies of social media influencers and found that followers attributed credibility, expertise, and trustworthiness to influencers at levels comparable to traditional celebrities. Djafarova and Rushworth (2017) examined Instagram influencer effects on young women's purchasing decisions and found that micro-influencers with smaller but more engaged followings had stronger effects on purchasing decisions than mega-influencers with larger but less engaged audiences. These studies established that influencer marketing is a real and consequential phenomenon.
Authenticity in Marketing
Authenticity is considered a key driver of influencer effectiveness. Audrezet, de Kerviler, and Moulard (2020) examined the role of authenticity in influencer marketing and found that perceived authenticity mediated the relationship between influencer transparency and follower engagement. Schouten, Janssen, and Verspaget (2020) compared the effectiveness of celebrity endorsers and social media influencers and found that influencers were perceived as more authentic and produced stronger parasocial relationships and purchase intentions. Both studies examined adults rather than teenagers.
Teenage Consumer Behavior
Research on teenage consumer behavior has examined peer influence and social comparison as drivers of purchasing decisions. Moschis and Churchill (1978) in a foundational study of consumer socialization found that teenagers are significantly influenced by peers in their consumption decisions. Patti and Chen (2009) examined the role of social media in teenage consumer behavior and found that social media use was associated with increased materialism and social comparison among teenagers. Neither study examined influencer marketing specifically.
Gap
There is a gap in the research because most influencer marketing studies have examined adult consumers rather than teenagers, and most studies have not directly measured the relationship between perceived authenticity and actual purchasing behavior in a teenage sample. This study fills that gap by surveying teenagers about their perceptions of influencer authenticity and their influencer-driven purchasing behavior.
Methodology
For my research I decided to ask some of my friends and classmates about their social media use and whether they buy things because of influencers. I made a quick survey on Google Forms with some questions about which influencers they follow, how authentic they think those influencers are, and whether they have bought things because an influencer recommended them. I shared the survey link in some group chats and got 12 people to fill it out. I know this is a small sample but it was hard to get people to respond. The survey had five questions. I looked at the answers and counted up how many people said yes to buying things based on influencer recommendations and compared that to how authentic they said they thought influencers were.
Results
Out of 12 people who answered my survey 9 said they follow at least one social media influencer. Of those 9 people 7 said they had bought something because an influencer recommended it. Of the 3 people who said they do not follow influencers none of them said they had bought something based on an influencer recommendation. When I asked people how authentic they thought influencers were on a scale of 1 to 5, the average for people who had bought things was 3.8 and the average for people who had not bought things was 2.9. This suggests that people who think influencers are more authentic are more likely to buy things they recommend which is what I expected to find.
Discussion
My results show that most of the teenagers I surveyed follow influencers and most of those who follow influencers have bought things based on influencer recommendations. The people who bought things rated influencers as more authentic on average than those who did not buy things. This is consistent with the research by Audrezet et al. and Schouten et al. that found authenticity is important for influencer effectiveness. However my sample of 12 people is too small to draw any real conclusions and I cannot generalize my findings to all teenagers.
Limitations
The biggest limitation is my sample size of 12 people who are all my friends and classmates. This is not a random sample and these people are all similar to each other in age, school, and social environment. I also did not use a validated questionnaire so my measures of authenticity and purchasing behavior may not be reliable. I did not get ethical approval for my study though I am not sure if that was required. The survey was anonymous but people might have answered differently if I asked them in person. I should have had more people take the survey.
Implications
Based on my findings I think schools should teach teenagers about influencer marketing so they can make more informed purchasing decisions. Parents should also talk to their teenagers about how influencers are often paid to promote products even when it seems like a personal recommendation.
Conclusion
My survey of 12 teenagers found that most follow influencers and most have bought things based on influencer recommendations. People who think influencers are more authentic seem more likely to make influencer-driven purchases. These findings are consistent with existing research on authenticity and influencer effectiveness but cannot be generalized due to the small sample size and convenience sampling. Future research should use a larger random sample and validated measures.
